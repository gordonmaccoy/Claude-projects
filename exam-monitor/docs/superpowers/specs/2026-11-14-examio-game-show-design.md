# Examio: teacher-controlled live game show

## Purpose
Make Examio review games feel like an in-person game show. Everyone looks at the classroom display for the question and answer choices; each phone is an uncluttered answer controller and personal results screen. Use the supplied references for rhythm and staging, not for copied branding, art or audio. Game Mode only, initially in Development. No changes to graded tests, libraries, quiz imports, scoring rules, ranking/tie rules, or reconnect identity. No publishing to Public as part of this change.

## Approaches
- **Teacher-controlled, selected:** the round opens and closes on a shared timer, but the teacher explicitly opens the scoreboard and then advances the game. This preserves time to discuss the correct answer.
- **Automatic scoreboard:** the vote chart would give way to the scoreboard after a fixed delay, with teacher control resuming for the next round. Less clicking, but the chart could disappear while the teacher is discussing it. Not selected.

## Round and screens
1. Launch opens the existing classroom lobby and shows the QR/PIN and large nickname/avatar tiles. Play original, mellow loop music there once sound is enabled; use the existing classroom volume/mute, with no autoplay promise and no duplicate audio on phones or host. Carry through a faded original classroom illustration with a high-contrast Examio dark-blue treatment.
2. A player taps a character to open a larger animated preview. Back leaves the confirmed selection unchanged; Confirm selects that character, not a separate sign-in. Make short seamless blink-and-idle loops for all 36 existing designs. Supply GIFs as requested; optimize delivery and provide stills for reduced motion. Use animated versions whenever a character appears in gameplay: confirmed/lobby state, personal results, scoreboard and podium. Keep the approved characters recognizable.
3. When a teacher starts or advances, use a short visual transition that does not consume reading or answering time. If the question has a media slide, leave it under teacher control as today; it comes before the reading phase. Show just the prompt (and its question image, if any) on the projection for five full seconds. No answer tiles, answer countdown or scoring during reading. The phone says to look at the classroom screen, without receiving the prompt or choice text. At the end, open the answer input and answer timer on the same authoritative server schedule. Use the full configured answer time and existing timer music during answering only.
4. In answering, the projection shows actual answer choices keyed to stable matching colors and symbols. The phone shows only large matching rectangles with shape and accessible button labels (e.g. Choice A, triangle), using available screen without scrolling. Retain the small question number, countdown, and own score; no quiz title, spectator rankings, prompt or answer text on phones. Respect the number of choices per game, including 2–5 choices, using only available slots and a fifth distinguishable symbol/color. Use ordinal slots for phone requests and views rather than stored option IDs, which could encode choice text. Enforce the answer window on the server and use the existing start-of-round roster, speed scoring and resume behavior.
5. Close once, either at deadline or after the last eligible answer is accepted. Stop round music and play a single original gong on the class display. Reveal the correct option and an animated aggregate vote chart by choice with count, retaining zero-vote choices and marking the correct answer in color and text/symbol. No named votes. Answer submission confirms receipt only; do not expose correctness or earned points before the round closes. Show the individual phone result: Correct, Wrong Answer, or Time’s Up (no accepted answer at timeout), with original graphics and only the player's own result. The phone’s running total and rank stay at their pre-round values until Show Scores. The chart stays until teacher taps Show Scores.
6. After **Show Scores**, animate visible totals from the prior score to the authoritative new score on both views. On the classroom board, animate one simultaneous reorder, based on final server ranks (not transient displayed numbers). Use existing board scope and tie rules. Do not replay round cues or misleading score changes on routine polling, stale snapshots, or refresh into a settled stage. A new positive outright leader retains a lighting/sound cue. The teacher decides when to tap **Next Question** or **Show Podium**; host fallback/permissions remain in place.
7. The final podium is staged in countdown order, third to first for available winners only, with spotlights and a final cue. Phones see only their own final score/rank. Reduce motion for those who request it and preserve legible final states. Sound stays local to the classroom volume control.

## Checks and builder latitude
Builder decides exact artwork, score-easing curve, interaction details and asset sizes; keep effects brief and keep the classroom text legible from the back of a room. Reuse the established real-time model and authentication, and enforce unique phase/question transitions across host and projector. Preserve same-browser host relay and fallback. Verify no prompt, choice text, answer key, or other players' data in student payloads, including reconnect and later phases. Exercise a synthetic multi-player game with media, early and time-expired rounds, 2/4/5 choices, wrong and missing answers, score crossover, ties, next/podium control and reload recovery. Verify a small phone viewport, keyboard usage, contrast, audio mute/autoplay and reduced motion. Do not send real student data or quiz documents to AI for testing.
